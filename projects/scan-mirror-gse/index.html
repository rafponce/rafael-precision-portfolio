NASA JPL / CASE STUDY 05

Precision Scan Mirror Ground Support Equipment

Accommodate assembly variation without imposing excessive reactions on a precision scan-mirror interface.

01

Problem

At NASA JPL, I worked on scan-mirror ground support equipment for the SBG-TIR / OTTER effort. The central design problem was accommodating a length variation of approximately ±5 mm without introducing undesirable loads into sensitive hardware.

An initially promising compliant-flexure concept exposed a more important question: where do the reaction forces and moments go when the assembly is brought into position?

02

03

Concepts

ITERATION / 01

01 / Compliant flexure

Allow elastic deflection to accommodate assembly variation. Study the geometry and the resulting interface reactions.

ITERATION / 02

02 / Three-piece torque bar

Pivot toward a segmented torque-bar arrangement for controlled torque and lower induced loads, informed by the flexure analysis.

04

Engineering Analysis

I used Siemens NX and NX Nastran to evaluate the flexure concept, including an enforced-displacement load case. Reaction forces and moments at the bolt interfaces helped reveal the risk of transferring unwanted loads to the mirror hardware.

The key decision was to change the mechanism concept when the load path proved problematic. Optimizing a local feature would not, by itself, resolve the system-level interface concern.

05

Design

The work moved from a compliant-flexure concept toward a three-piece torque-bar design. The intent was to control applied torque and reduce the loads induced by assembly accommodation.

Related opto-mechanical work included precision alignment and assembly fixtures, NX modeling, and ISO 10110 optical drawings. These tasks reinforced the connection between the model, the drawing, and the physical assembly.

06

Build / Test

This case study describes design and analysis work. It does not claim that the scan-mirror GSE was flight hardware or that the final torque-bar concept completed qualification testing.

Assembly feasibility and interface behavior guided the iterations. Detailed company drawings and original analysis outputs are not reproduced here.

07

Results

The most important outcome was recognizing the interface-load risk early and changing the design direction. The engineering decision prioritized the behavior of the complete assembly.

08

What I Learned

A flexible component can still produce harmful loads at its constraints. I learned to inspect reactions and moments, question the assumed load path, and change the architecture when geometry optimization alone would not solve the problem.
