01

Problem

For ME133, I am exploring a small self-balancing cube or triangular body using an Arduino UNO R4 and an IMU. The eventual goal is to move onto a corner or edge and maintain balance.

The immediate engineering question is how to define a controllable first prototype before attempting the complete self-righting behavior. This case study is a project brief, not a completed result.

ITERATION / 02

Planar triangular architecture

Consider a simpler one-axis body to reduce the number of coupled variables during the initial controls experiment.

04

Engineering Analysis

The planned analysis begins with a free-body diagram, center-of-mass location, gravitational moment, and available actuator torque. The actuation architecture has not yet been selected.

A useful first test will separate sensor estimation from control performance. Timing, bias, and motor saturation will need to be measured on the actual system.

05

Design

The mechanical design is still in development. The Arduino UNO R4 and IMU provide the starting point, while the frame, motor, driver, and power system remain design decisions.

A sensible proposed sequence is sensing first, constrained actuation second, and free balancing after the individual subsystems behave predictably.

06

Build / Test

No completed prototype or measured balance performance is claimed here. The next milestone is a benchtop IMU and actuator check with documented sampling and response behavior.

The proposed test plan will measure attitude error, recovery time, and actuator saturation under repeatable disturbances. Numerical acceptance limits are still to be defined.

08

What I Learned

The current design lesson is to narrow the first experiment. Demonstrating a well-instrumented one-axis response will provide more useful information than committing early to a complicated mechanism.
