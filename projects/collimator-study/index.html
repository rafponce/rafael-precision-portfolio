Optomechanical Collimator Misalignment Study

Explore image-position sensitivity to optical tilt and the stress response of a custom lens mount under asymmetric loading.

01

Problem

I wanted to understand how mechanical alignment decisions appear in an optical model. I built a basic single-lens collimator in Zemax and designed a corresponding lens mount in SolidWorks.

The study examined a 1° coordinate-break tilt in the optical setup and, separately, an asymmetric load on the mechanical mount.

04

Engineering Analysis

The spot-diagram screenshots report an on-axis image position of 0.000 mm in the aligned case and 2.651 mm after the 1° tilt. The off-axis image positions shift by a similar amount.

The displayed RMS and geometric radii are similar at the shown precision. The most clearly supported result is image-position sensitivity; these screenshots do not establish a quantified increase in spot size.

The separate SolidWorks static load case reports a maximum von Mises stress of approximately 0.277 MPa. No displacement-to-optical-model transfer is documented, so the 10 N load is not presented as a calibrated cause of the imposed 1° optical tilt.

05

Design

The mount was modeled around a 25 mm lens, with a central bore and three bolt holes. The CAD figure shows the original model used in the study.

The mechanical boundary conditions fixed two bolt holes while loading the third, creating an asymmetric structural response for inspection.

06

Build / Test

This was a simulation project. The supplied presentation documents Zemax comparisons, mount CAD, and a SolidWorks static stress result.

A next step would be to extract mount translations and rotations, transfer them into the optical model, and validate the relationship with a physical alignment test.

07

Results

The study connected optical alignment sensitivity with lens-mount design. It established separate optical and structural baselines for a future coupled analysis.

08

What I Learned

Optical performance and mechanical stress answer different questions. A low stress result alone does not establish acceptable alignment. A stronger next iteration would connect actual structural deformation to the optical model.
