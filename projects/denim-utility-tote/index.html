Denim Utility Tote

Turn a pair of jeans into a usable tote while keeping the original pockets as functional storage.

01

Problem

I wanted to turn an existing pair of jeans into a practical bag while keeping the useful details already built into the garment.

The waistband, belt loops, and original pockets became part of the tote’s design rather than discarded material.

02

Requirements

RequirementEngineering definition
ReuseUse the jeans as the primary material source.
UtilityRetain the original pockets for storage.
CarryAdd handles for normal tote use.
FinishClose and shape the lower edge into a usable bag body.
03

Concepts

ITERATION / 01

Preserve useful features

Keep the original pocket arrangement and upper-garment details.

ITERATION / 02

Reassign the material

Use the remaining denim to form the tote body and carry handles.

04

Engineering Analysis

The design problem was primarily one of layout and construction: retain accessible pockets, form a usable opening, and connect the handles to the bag body.

The completed tote retains accessible pockets on both sides and two carry handles.

05

Design

The finished tote keeps the recognizable waistband, belt loops, denim seams, and original pockets.

The carry straps turn the repurposed garment into a bag while the existing pockets add organized storage.

06

Build / Test

I cut and sewed the jeans into a tote and retained the original pockets for utility.

Both supplied images document the finished bag, showing the two sides and handles.

07

Results

A pair of jeans became a usable tote with its original pockets preserved for utility.

08

What I Learned

Upcycling works best when existing features become part of the design. Keeping the pockets preserved both function and the character of the original garment.
